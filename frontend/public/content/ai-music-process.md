# How the AI Music Gets Made

Every track on viz-vibes is AI-generated. People always ask what that actually means, so here's a look at the process.

## The tools

I use a combination of AI music generation models. The workflow usually looks like this:

1. **Prompt** — describe the vibe, genre, tempo, mood
2. **Generate** — let the model produce several variations
3. **Curate** — pick the best ones, sometimes regenerate sections
4. **Polish** — light mastering for consistent volume and EQ

The whole process for a single track takes anywhere from 20 minutes to a few hours, depending on how picky I'm being.

## What makes a good prompt

Specificity helps. "A chill lo-fi track" gives you something generic. But "a lo-fi hip-hop beat with vinyl crackle, soft Rhodes piano, and a melancholy chord progression in D minor at 75 BPM" gets you somewhere interesting.

I've found that **referencing textures and feelings** works better than referencing specific artists:

- *"warm and hazy, like sunlight through dusty curtains"*
- *"mechanical but organic, like a clock made of wood"*
- *"the feeling of driving alone at 2am"*

## The curation problem

AI music models can generate *a lot* of content. The hard part isn't generation — it's selection. For every track that makes it onto viz-vibes, I've probably listened to 20–30 variations and discarded most of them.

The tracks that work tend to have:

- **A clear emotional arc** — they go somewhere
- **Interesting textures** — not just standard synth patches
- **Space** — room for the listener (and the visualizer) to breathe

## The ethics question

Is it "real" music? I think so, in the same way that a photograph is a real image even though a camera did most of the work. The creative decisions — what to generate, what to keep, how to sequence it — are human decisions.

But I understand why people feel differently about it. That's part of why every page on viz-vibes clearly states that the music is AI-generated. Transparency matters.

## What's next

I'm experimenting with longer-form compositions — pieces that evolve over 5–10 minutes instead of the typical 2–3 minute loop. The challenge is maintaining coherence over that duration. AI models tend to lose the thread after a while.

I'm also interested in **interactive generation** — what if the visualizer could influence the music in real time, not just the other way around? That's a bigger technical challenge, but it's the dream.
